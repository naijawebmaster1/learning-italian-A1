What is the Definite Article?

Like "the" in English — but Italian has 7 forms depending on gender, number, and the first letter of the noun.

In Italian, every noun has a gender (masculine or feminine) and a number (singular or plural). The definite article must agree with the noun in both. Unlike English, which has only one word ("the"), Italian has 7 different forms.

Key Rules to Remember

🔤 lo vs il

Use lo / gli before: s+consonant, z, ps, gn, x, y. Otherwise use il / i.

✂️ Elision with l'

Both lo (masc.) and la (fem.) become l' before a vowel. E.g. l'amica, l'ombrello.

📚 Plural of lo

lo and l' (masc.) both become gli in plural. E.g. gli studenti, gli amici.

♀ Feminine Plural

All feminine articles (la and l') become le in plural. le case, le idee.

📞 Invariable nouns

Some nouns don't change in plural (e.g. il cinema → i cinema). The article still must agree!

🎓 Gender tip

Nouns ending in -o are usually masculine; -a feminine; -e can be either — check the article!
